Hands-on from day one

Stop reading about computers.
Start operating one.

This is a practical, task-based course. Every lesson hands you a real computer and a real job to complete — no long lectures, no slides to memorise. You learn to use a PC the same way you'd learn to ride a bicycle: by doing it, falling off, and doing it again until your hands know the way.

Built for someone touching a PC for real, on purpose

Whether you've never turned on a computer or you get by clicking around nervously, this course meets you where you are. There's no assumed background — only a willingness to sit at a keyboard and do the exercise in front of you.

You'll practise on more than one machine wherever possible. A skill you can only perform on "your" computer isn't mastered yet — a skill you can perform on any computer, with a different keyboard, a different screen, maybe even a different operating system, is the real target.

By the end of this course you will be able to

  • Operate any Windows or Mac computer confidently, without help
  • Manage files and folders like a filing clerk, not a hoarder
  • Write, calculate and present using real office software
  • Get online safely and communicate professionally by email
  • Diagnose and fix the ten most common everyday PC problems
  • Sit down at an unfamiliar computer and be productive in minutes

How the course is structured

Three phases, twelve weeks, one skill added on top of the last. Every week lives on its own page inside its phase folder, so you always know exactly where to look.

Weeks 1–4

Phase 1 — Digital Foundations

Turning the machine on, controlling it with confidence, and organising files without losing anything.

Weeks 5–8

Phase 2 — Productivity & Communication

Documents, spreadsheets, slides, email and cloud storage — the tools of everyday work.

Weeks 9–12

Phase 3 — Advanced Skills & Troubleshooting

System settings, the command line, fixing your own problems, and a final practical exam.

Every lesson follows the same practical method

1

Basic task, on your own computer

You perform the core skill, step by step, on the machine in front of you, until it's ticked off.

2

Advanced task, same skill area

A harder version of the same job — the kind a real task at work or school would actually ask of you.

3

Prove it, on a different computer

You repeat the task on a classmate's PC, a lab machine, or another OS, so the skill travels with you.

Keep this site open while you work

The curriculum page is your map for all twelve weeks — bookmark it. Each week's lesson page has checklists that save your progress in your own browser, so you can tick a task, close the tab, and come back to exactly where you left off.

Scientific notation

4.5 × 106

Write huge and tiny numbers in a short, standard form. Read each part, try the labs, then practise with instant feedback.

1. What is scientific notation?

Scientific notation writes very large or very small numbers in a shorter form: a × 10n

  • a is at least 1 and less than 10 (1 ≤ a < 10)
  • n is an integer (whole number, positive, negative or zero)
  • 10n shows how many places the decimal point moved

4,500,000 = 4.5 × 106 and 0.00032 = 3.2 × 10−4

Writing it: right and wrong

Right

  • 4.5 × 106
  • 3.2 × 10−4
  • 1 × 103
  • 9.99 × 100

Wrong

  • 45 × 105 (45 is not below 10)
  • 0.5 × 107 (0.5 is below 1)
  • 3.2 × 104 for 0.00032 (sign is wrong)
  • 4.5 x 10^6 in a final answer (use × and a raised power)
Read-aloud script

Scientific notation is a short way to write very big or very small numbers. We write a number a, times ten to the power n. The number a must be at least one and less than ten. The power n must be a whole number. For example, four point five times ten to the power six equals four million, five hundred thousand.

2. Why do we use it?

Earth to the Sun: 149,600,000 km = 1.496 × 108 km.
A bacterium: 0.000002 m = 2 × 10−6 m.

It helps with numbers that are extremely large, extremely small, hard to read, hard to calculate with, or common in science.

Lab: zoom along the powers of ten

Read-aloud script

The distance from Earth to the Sun is one hundred forty-nine million, six hundred thousand kilometres. In scientific notation it is one point four nine six times ten to the power eight. A bacterium is two times ten to the power negative six metres long.

3. The basic structure

Two parts: the coefficient a and the power of ten 10n.

Valid coefficients: 1, 2.5, 3.72, 9.999. Not valid: 0.5, 12, 25.6.

So 12 × 105 must become 1.2 × 106.

Read-aloud script

Every number in scientific notation has a coefficient and a power of ten. The coefficient must be at least one and less than ten. Twelve times ten to the five is not correct. We rewrite it as one point two times ten to the six.

4. The exponent and its laws

A positive exponent gives a large number. A negative exponent gives a small number.
5.2 × 104 = 52,000 and 5.2 × 10−4 = 0.00052

Laws of exponents

LawRuleExample
Multiply10a × 10b = 10a+b104 × 105 = 109
Divide10a ÷ 10b = 10a−b107 ÷ 103 = 104
Power of a power(10a)b = 10ab(103)2 = 106
Zero exponent100 = 16.3 = 6.3 × 100
Negative exponent10−n = 1 ÷ 10n10−3 = 1 ÷ 1000 = 0.001
Read-aloud script

The exponent tells us how many places the decimal point moves. Positive means a large number. Negative means a small number. To multiply powers of ten, add the exponents. To divide, subtract them. A power of a power multiplies the exponents. Ten to the zero is one. Ten to the negative n is one divided by ten to the n.

5. Powers of 10

Lab: decimal mover

Type any number (for example 0.0000072 or 45000) and see how the decimal point moves.

Read-aloud script

Ten to the one is ten. Ten to the two is one hundred. Each step adds one zero. Going the other way, ten to the negative one is zero point one, and each step down moves the decimal point one place further.

6. Large numbers to scientific notation

Example: write 45,000. Start with 45,000.0 and move the decimal point until one non-zero digit is in front: 4.5. It moved 4 places left, so the answer is 4.5 × 104.

Check: 4.5 × 10,000 = 45,000. Correct.

Another: 7,250,000 → 7.25, six places left, so 7.25 × 106.

Rule: large number, decimal moves left, exponent positive.

Read-aloud script

To write a large number, move the decimal point left until one non-zero digit is in front of it. Count the moves. That count is a positive exponent. Forty-five thousand becomes four point five times ten to the four.

7. Small numbers to scientific notation

Many mistakes happen here. Take 0.00045. Move the decimal point to get 4.5: 4 places right. The number was below 1, so the exponent is negative: 4.5 × 10−4.

Worked: 0.0000072 moves 6 places right to 7.2, so 7.2 × 10−6. Check: 7.2 × 0.000001 = 0.0000072.

Memory aid: left is positive, right is negative.

Read-aloud script

To write a small number, move the decimal point right until one non-zero digit is in front of it. Count the moves. Because the number was less than one, the exponent is negative. Zero point zero zero zero zero zero seven two becomes seven point two times ten to the negative six.

8. Back to ordinary form

3.6 × 105: exponent +5, move the decimal 5 places right: 360,000.

4.8 × 10−4: exponent −4, move the decimal 4 places left: 0.00048.

Examples: 6.2 × 103 = 6,200 · 9.3 × 10−3 = 0.0093 · 2.75 × 10−5 = 0.0000275

Read-aloud script

To go back to ordinary form, look at the sign of the exponent. Positive: move the decimal point right. Negative: move it left. The size of the exponent is the number of places.
